Small and mid-size medtech companies and regulatory consulting firms are navigating the most significant regulatory transition in EU medical device history — with lean teams, limited budgets, and deadlines that cannot move.

The shift from the Medical Device Directive (MDD) to EU MDR 2017/745, and from the In Vitro Diagnostic Directive (IVDD) to EU IVDR 2017/746, has created a compliance landscape of extraordinary complexity. Most organisations responding to this transition were managing it across disconnected spreadsheets, shared drives, and email threads — with no single view of where they stood, no audit trail, and no reporting that worked for senior management or a Notified Body.

Four specific pain points drove the design of this tool:

**1. No unified compliance position.**
Teams lacked a structured baseline covering the full scope of MDR and IVDR obligations. Gap assessments existed in isolated files or consultants' heads, not in a shared, queryable record.

**2. Regulatory updates slipping through.**
The MDR/IVDR transition has seen multiple extensions, phased deadlines, and Commission proposals for targeted simplification — most recently COM(2025) 1023 in December 2025. Without a live tracking mechanism, critical changes were easy to miss.

**3. Nothing ready to present.**
Management reviews under ISO 13485:2016 Clause 5.6 require documented compliance reporting. Building that from raw gap data, on deadline, without a dedicated tool, is a recurring and expensive problem.

**4. No audit trail.**
Any change to a compliance record — a status update, a revised deadline, a new gap item — should be attributable, timestamped, and traceable. Spreadsheet environments offer none of this.
